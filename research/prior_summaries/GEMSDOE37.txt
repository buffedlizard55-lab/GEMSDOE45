Session findings · 2026-10-05 Five results that produced this build 1. Binary emission is provably optimal Because FN w = |G| − TP w , the official index simplifies to DTI = TP w /(0.8|G| + 0.2 TP w + 0.2 FP w ) , which strictly increases when every predicted value is scaled up. For a fixed set of predicted cells, soft probabilities can only lose. This file emits exactly 1.0 or 0.0. 2. The 0.2778 map was read, not assumed We downloaded the highest-scoring historical raster and measured it: 37,654 binary dots, perfect dotting (local mass 1.000), minimum 2.236 px off-catalogue. Its score came from emission geometry . The detector was therefore the remaining lever. 3. A learned physics ranker beats the old surface 94 label-free features — raw bands, multi-scale gradient and Hessian-ridge responses, scales-survived counts, and H0 superlevel-set persistence per physics family — fitted with gradient boosting. On a blocked holdout it scores 0.2486 against 0.1530 for the unsupervised persistence consensus, which is itself below the 0.1957 random floor. 4. Catalogue features degenerate by construction Training positives are the catalogue, so a distance-to-catalogue feature separates them perfectly and the booster discards all 94 physics columns. The physics+geometry and geometry-only arms come out bit-identical at 0.0688 versus 0.1973. Label-free features are not a style choice here; they are a requirement. 5. Unmapped faults sit about a kilometre off the map Hiding a third of the 3,199 catalogue segments per fold: only 7.3–7.9% of hidden fault cells lie within 300 m of a visible fault (base rate 5.8–6.0%), 27–30% within 600 m, median distance ~1 km. That is the measured reason a 3 px stand-off beats 2 px, 0 px, 4 px and 6 px at every budget tested. Emission geometry, swept not guessed 33 configurations: stand-off 3 px wins everywhere, 2.9 px dot spacing beats 3.5 px everywhere, and the budget plateau runs 60k–100k with a peak at 80,000 dots. The winner leads in all three folds individually. Honest negative result, repeated with a cleaner protocol: multi-scale persistence used on its own as a ranking is no better than random placement. Persistence earns its place here as one feature family among 94 and as a cross-scale stability certificate — not as a standalone fault detector. Not comparable, and recorded as such: historical maps score near zero on this holdout only because their stand-off was measured against the full catalogue, which puts every one of their dots beyond the 300 m kernel from the hidden segments. How we work Evidence before confidence What this project will do Use official challenge rules and disclose what data came from unauthenticated mirrors. Preserve a fixed hypothesis, detector, and promotion gate before looking at raster outcomes. Compare candidates against the public catalogue under spatial separation and an equal pixel budget. Make the TIFF fail closed on any output-format or promotion-gate failure. What it will not claim That public catalogue associations reproduce expert fault labels or hidden scoring. That persistent topographic/geophysical edges prove mineralization. That an unverified historical submission is an organizer-confirmed score. That a good pseudo-holdout result guarantees a top leaderboard position.
