The training labels in the data tab are the existing USGS Quaternary compilation, but both prize phases score the expert-mapped new fault dataset. A submission that reproduces the catalogue earns credit only where the experts' new labels coincide with it, and every DTI printed elsewhere on this site is a monitor against the wrong population. That asymmetry is also why the emission width was measured rather than assumed: the adopted policy is a thinned skeleton (width 0 px) at floor 0.1 — at that floor the measured width curve peaks at 0 px on every ensemble, so a wider band would lower the score, not raise it — see the width measurement . Where evidence is missing, the page says so. The task Predict where geological faults are in the GeoDAWN region of northwestern Nevada — faults
being surface indicators of geothermal resources. The submission is a single-band float32 GeoTIFF of
per-pixel probabilities on the competition grid
( submission format ). The twist that shapes everything: the public USGS fault database is incomplete . Scoring
uses a private set of newly-expert-labelled faults absent from that database, and after the
initial round an expert panel reviews every team's predictions to expand the label set for a final
re-scoring ( competition structure ). Predicting the known
faults back is therefore not the goal; finding the unmapped ones is.
