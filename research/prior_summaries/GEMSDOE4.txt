The training labels in the data tab are the existing USGS Quaternary compilation, but both prize phases score the expert-mapped new fault dataset. A submission that reproduces the catalogue earns credit only where the experts' new labels coincide with it, and every DTI printed elsewhere on this site is a monitor against the wrong population. That asymmetry is also why the emission width was measured rather than assumed: the adopted policy is a thinned skeleton (width 0 px) at floor 0.1 — at that floor the measured width curve peaks at 0 px on every ensemble, so a wider band would lower the score, not raise it — see the width measurement . GEMSDOE4 — the new-fault-first line The repository's earlier lines selected their emission policy on the catalogue population. The rules score the new faults in both prize phases (quoted verbatim above and
on Metric ), so GEMSDOE4 is a different strategy rather than another
variant of the same model: Lineament features instead of raw bands. Multi-scale Sato ridgeness, structure-tensor
      coherence and windowed context on the 6 bands that carry an edge signal
      ( src/lineament_features.py , 63 features), fed to a HistGradientBoostingClassifier . A tree model can split on "this pixel sits on a
      2 km line"; it cannot invent that from 19 uncorrelated band values. Supervision from an independent fault compilation. The catalogue teaches the model the
      faults geologists already mapped — the ones with the most obvious geophysical
      expression. Adding the SGMC-derived proxy labels
      ( data/evidence/proxy/proxy_catalogue.tif ) gives it examples of faults the
      catalogue does not contain, which is the population the metric rewards. The
      catalogue-only ablation is a measured number, not an assumption
      ( --no-proxy-labels ). Selection on the new-fault population, on geography the model never saw. Fold 0
      selects the emission rule by the proxy DTI over its own blocks; fold 1 measures it and is
      never scored by the sweep. The catalogue DTI is reported next to it, never used to select. A union of structurally different detectors. Coverage is cheap under this metric (FN
      carries β = 0.8, FP only α = 0.2), so detectors whose errors differ are worth more
      together than apart: the 11-fold deep ensemble, the classical raw-band GBM, and three lineament
      NFF detectors - two at different seeds on the same population and one supervised on the
      independent compilation's own pixels only ( --supervision proxy_only ), whose errors
      are structurally anti-correlated with every catalogue-trained member. Halving is only exact if the in-sample boost is the same kind of boost on both fold sets (B_01 = B_23); the two groups are built so the fold-difficulty term cancels exactly, and the residual is exposure - with that symmetry assumption as its only gap, stated here rather than hidden. in-sample exposure is 0.0543 . A policy selected on a scope that is in-sample for part of the member pool is selected on a mixture the sweep cannot claim. The clean pool is the set of members whose holdout IS the union's scope; see data/evidence/combined_clean/report.json. Caveat, stated here rather than left to the reader. In-sample-ness does not make a member a bad detector - the private scored set is a different population of faults, not different geography. It makes the MEASUREMENT optimistic, and therefore any policy chosen with it. The emission budget: what a higher score actually costs The official metric collapses to a two-term identity, verified numerically against src.metrics.GtContext on every scope below (max relative error 2.1e-16 ): DTI = TP_w / (alpha*(TP_w + FP_w) + beta*|G| + eps) the prediction enters only through the weighted coverage TP_w and the weighted false-positive mass FP_w; one unit of coverage is worth beta/alpha = 4 units of false-positive mass - with
β/α = 4.0, one more unit of coverage is worth four units of

Where evidence is missing, the page says so. The task Predict where geological faults are in the GeoDAWN region of northwestern Nevada — faults
being surface indicators of geothermal resources. The submission is a single-band float32 GeoTIFF of
per-pixel probabilities on the competition grid
( submission format ). The twist that shapes everything: the public USGS fault database is incomplete . Scoring
uses a private set of newly-expert-labelled faults absent from that database, and after the
initial round an expert panel reviews every team's predictions to expand the label set for a final
re-scoring ( competition structure ). Predicting the known
faults back is therefore not the goal; finding the unmapped ones is.
