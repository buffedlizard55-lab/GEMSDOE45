What it is

Every one of the catalogue's 3,866 mapped fault tips is extended by its own length, drawn by inverse transform from a Kaplan–Meier product-limit estimate (Kaplan & Meier 1958) of the catalogue's own along-strike relay gaps. The file adds nothing on the mapped catalogue and nothing within 2 px (200 m) of it — the exclusion radius that the family's live leaderboard calibrated — and then keeps only the dots that pay for themselves under the metric's own marginal rule.

Why this design, in one paragraph each

Why a survival function and not a radius

A fixed radius asserts that every fault extends the same distance past its mapped tip. The catalogue itself refutes that: measured over its own tips, the along-strike distance to the nearest other mapped segment has median 9 px and a heavy tail (S(1) = 0.896 but S(30) = 0.346). The product-limit estimator is the non-parametric answer to "how far does a fault run on past where the mapper stopped", and it is fitted from data this competition ships, not from an assumption. Each tip gets its own draw, so the extension length is a random variable per fault — which the build asserts (realised_distinct >= 5) rather than claims.

Why splays and parallel strands are in the candidate set

DrivenData staff defined a new fault as "any fault pixel not already captured by USGS/INGENIOUS", explicitly including "newly mapped geometry of an existing fault system". A splay or a parallel strand drawn between grid nodes is exactly that, and it is where a reconnaissance compilation is thinnest.

Why the dots are binary and why they are chosen greedily

Because TP_w is a maximum over predictions near each truth pixel while FP_w is a sum over emitted mass, scaling a prediction down can only hurt: the optimal emission is binary. Selection is then budgeted maximum coverage with a submodular objective, for which greedy is the standard (1 − 1/e) approximation, stopped by the metric's own marginal condition w > 0.2·DTI evaluated at the portfolio's best live-observed DTI (0.2778).

Limitations

  1. It is not validated against the hidden truth, and no such validation is possible here.
  2. The 1 m lidar DEM was not used; the download links are behind the competition login and the hosts are unreachable from this sandbox (HTTP 000).
  3. No independent off-catalogue fault inventory. The two free official candidates — USGS QFAULT (earthquake.usgs.gov/ws/qfault) and the USGS State Geologic Map Compilation (mrdata.usgs.gov/geology/state) — were checked for obtainability and failed here. This is the single most valuable missing input.
  4. The candidate field is a corroboration product, not a trained detector. It was measured against the live ladder and does not predict it; the honest reading is that the evidence planes supplied for this competition do not separate mapped from unmapped faults at the kernel's scale.
  5. Owner-reported scores only. 0.2778, 0.3345 and every other leaderboard figure quoted here are claims from the GEMSDOE family or a third-party snapshot; none has an organizer receipt.
